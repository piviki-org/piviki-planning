# GDPR baseline digest r3-1: transactional email providers

Researched 2026-11-03. All claims below come from documents fetched in this run, unless a line says "unverified". Quotes are verbatim or close paraphrase.

## Lettermint (Lettermint B.V., Zwolle, NL)

- Art. 28 DPA: the DPA "applies automatically whenever Lettermint processes Controller Personal Data". It is part of the T&C. A countersigned copy is available on request. The Art. 28(3) description (Sec. 3.1) lists subject matter, duration, data categories and special-category handling | https://lettermint.co/dpa | Lettermint B.V. | Version of 9 Oct 2026 (applies to existing customers from 8 Nov 2026; 17 Jul 2026 version until then) | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Sub-processors, Sec. 7.2: Lettermint must "actively inform Controller in writing, by email or an in-product notice... at least thirty (30) calendar days before the Subprocessor begins processing". Sec. 7.3: Controller "may object... on reasonable grounds" within that period, and the new sub-processor cannot process data before the period ends. Sec. 7.4: if the objection is not resolved, either party may terminate the affected Services | https://lettermint.co/dpa | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Sub-processor list: UpCloud (hosting, NL), OVH Cloud (encrypted backups, DE) and Postmastery (deliverability monitoring, NL) are the only parties that handle email content. Aikido (security scanning, "Europe") and Mistral (AI inference for support/abuse, "Europe") are listed as "No email access". The page says "Only UpCloud, OVH Cloud and Postmastery handle the emails... all within the EU" | https://lettermint.co/subprocessors | Lettermint B.V. | last updated 29 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- EU-only by contract, Sec. 8.1: "hosting, storage, and processing of Email Data within Lettermint's European email infrastructure and service-Subprocessor chain shall take place within the European Union". Sec. 8.2: other Controller data, "including limited information used for support or abuse handling", stays in the EU/EEA. Sec. 8.4: no transfer "or permit remote access... outside the EEA" unless the controller authorised it under Sec. 7/16, Chapter V GDPR is met, and a TIA/supplementary measures are in place. Sec. 8.3: carve-out for transmission to destinations the controller selects (recipient mail servers, webhooks) | https://lettermint.co/dpa | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Breach notice, Sec. 10.1: "without undue delay and in any event within twenty-four (24) hours after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach". The required contents are in Sec. 10.2 and phased notification in Sec. 10.3 | https://lettermint.co/dpa | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Retention (Sec. 3.1 Duration): message content, attachments, recipient addresses, metadata and events are kept for 28 days or a period the controller configures. Analytics events are kept no longer than 90 days. After termination (Sec. 11.2–11.5) there is a 30-day retrieval window, then deletion. Backup residue lasts at most 14 days. The sub-processor page says logs are "normally kept for no more than 14 days" | https://lettermint.co/dpa ; https://lettermint.co/subprocessors | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 / 29 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Audit, Sec. 12.3: Lettermint "shall permit and contribute to audits", run by the controller or an independent auditor, including on-site inspection where reasonably necessary. Conditions: 30 days' notice (Sec. 12.5), normally one audit per 12 months (Sec. 12.7), each party bears its own costs (Sec. 12.8). Sec. 12.4 says the final choice of audit method "remains with Controller" | https://lettermint.co/dpa | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Government access, Sec. 5.8: for binding public-authority requests, Lettermint will, unless prohibited, (a) notify the controller before disclosure, (b) review legality and scope, (c) challenge where appropriate, and (d) disclose the minimum required | https://lettermint.co/dpa | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- AI/LLM, Sec. 13.1–13.4: AI may be used for support-ticket triage and abuse handling. The AI provider counts as a Sub-processor, and Sec. 8 governs location. Data is not used for model training, and provider retention is minimised. The list shows Mistral as the AI provider, located in "Europe" | https://lettermint.co/dpa ; /subprocessors | Lettermint B.V. | v. 9 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Weakness: the sub-processor page has a generic "International Data Transfers" section that mentions SCCs and BCRs for transfers outside the EEA. This is boilerplate and contradicts nothing in DPA Sec. 8. Separately, Stripe and other payment providers process billing data as controller-side data, outside the DPA (Sec. 2.3) | https://lettermint.co/subprocessors | Lettermint B.V. | 29 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | compliance

## Scaleway (Scaleway SAS, Paris; Iliad group)

- Transactional Email (TEM) Specific Conditions v. 07/04/2026 contain no data-protection clauses. They cover service description, prohibited use and billing. Sec. 4.3: "Sent messages are transmitted and then automatically deleted after processing". Sec. 2.1: Scaleway makes "sending data and technical logs of the TEM Service hosted on its infrastructure" available to the client. No log retention period is stated | https://www-uploads.scaleway.com/Specific_Conditions_Transactionnal_Email_Services_v042026_3f5487a93c.pdf | Scaleway SAS | v. 07/04/2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Current DPA not retrieved: scaleway.com/en/contracts and /en/subprocessorlist return 403, and the archived contracts page is JS-rendered with no PDF link. The only DPA text obtained is a third-party mirror whose PDF metadata is dated 13 Aug 2021. It may be out of date, so every DPA claim below is "2021 version, unverified as current" | https://sofus.dk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Scaleway-DPA.pdf | Scaleway SAS (mirror) | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | compliance
- (2021 DPA) Art. 28: a DPA exists and forms part of the Contract. Art. 7.1 gives a "general authorisation to subcontract". No notice period or objection right appears in that text | sofus.dk mirror | Scaleway | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | compliance
- (2021 DPA) Art. 11.1(b): Scaleway undertakes "not to send, disseminate or store Personal Data in a country outside the European Union without the Client's express prior agreement". The exception is transfers required by Union or Member State law, with prior notice to the client | sofus.dk mirror | Scaleway | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | compliance
- (2021 DPA) Art. 9.1(a): breach notice to the client "as quickly as possible after having become aware of it". No hour figure is given | sofus.dk mirror | Scaleway | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | compliance
- (2021 DPA) Art. 12.2: audit with 15 days' written notice, at the client's expense, no more than once a year, by an independent auditor "appointed by Scaleway". Art. 13: deletion at the end of the contract | sofus.dk mirror | Scaleway | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | compliance
- Sub-processor list (Wayback snapshot of 14 Jun 2026, page "Last reviewed: July 2025"): by agreeing to the DPA, the customer gives "general authorization to add, modify or remove sub-processors from this list". There is a "Notification of updates" link to a change history. No notice period or objection right is stated. Data centres include US-headquartered Equinix EMEA, Iron Mountain and Digital Realty, all with processing location in the EU. TEM is not named as a product with a specific sub-processor | https://web.archive.org/web/20260614195105/https://www.scaleway.com/en/subprocessorlist/ | Scaleway SAS | reviewed Jul 2025 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | compliance
- Group: the 2021 DPA gives the DPO contact as dpo@iliad.fr, which points to Iliad group involvement. Whether Iliad group companies access data is unverified | sofus.dk mirror | Scaleway | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-03 | low | compliance

## Amazon SES / AWS

- Art. 28 DPA: the AWS Data Processing Addendum supplements the AWS Customer Agreement. No version date was found in the extracted text | https://d1.awsstatic.com/legal/aws-dpa/aws-dpa.pdf | Amazon Web Services | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Region, Sec. 12.1 (verified text): "Customer can specify the location(s) where Customer Data will be processed within the AWS Network (each a 'Region'), including Regions in the EEA. Once Customer has made its choice, AWS will not transfer Customer Data from Customer's selected Region(s) except as necessary to provide the Services initiated by Customer, or as necessary to comply with the law or valid and binding order of a governmental body." This is a commitment that follows from region selection, with two open exceptions. It is not an EU-only promise. The brief's draft wording ("will not access or use, or disclose") is actually from Sec. 3, Confidentiality, not the region clause | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Sec. 12.2: SCCs (2021/914) apply to transfers to third countries. Sec. 12.3: SCCs are displaced if AWS adopts BCRs or "an alternative recognized compliance standard" | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Government access, Sec. 3: AWS will not access, use or disclose Customer Data except to provide the Services "or as necessary to comply with the law or a valid and binding order of a governmental body (such as a subpoena or court order)". AWS will try to redirect the authority to the customer, may give the authority the customer's basic contact information, and will give reasonable notice "unless AWS is legally prohibited". The DPA does not name the CLOUD Act. Whether the CLOUD Act applies is unverified this run | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Breach, Sec. 9.1: AWS will "notify Customer of a Security Incident without undue delay after becoming aware". No hour or day figure is given | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Sub-processors, Sec. 6.1: there is a published list. "At least 30 days before AWS engages a Sub-processor, AWS will update the applicable website and provide Customer with a mechanism to obtain notice." The only ways to object are to terminate, stop using the service, or move data to another Region. There is no veto | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- The sub-processor page lists AWS group entities as sellers/providers of SES for customers in Japan and Singapore. It includes "AWS entities providing service improvement", which apply "unless customer opts out". It says some messaging services (End User Messaging, SNS) "may utilize downstream providers to route and process data globally". Email notification of changes is opt-in by subscription | https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/sub-processors/ | AWS | last updated 30 Oct 2026 | accessed 2026-11-03 | medium | compliance
- Audit, Sec. 10.2–10.3: AWS chooses external auditors, who audit at least annually to ISO 27001. The customer gets the report on written request under an NDA. Inspection is mentioned only in passing: if AWS does not follow an audit or inspection instruction, the customer may terminate. Customer on-site audit is not granted as a right | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
- Deletion, Sec. 14: AWS returns or deletes data when the customer uses Service Controls, up to the Termination Date and for 90 days after it | aws-dpa.pdf | AWS | undated | accessed 2026-11-03 | high | compliance
